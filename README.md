# RNA sequencing data from human corneal epithelial cells cultured on dECM

This repository is maintained by Zongjin Li Lab for the RNA sequencing study of human corneal epithelial cells cultured on decellularized extracellular matrix (dECM).
  
  Keratoconus (KC) is characterized by progressive corneal thinning, biomechanical weakening, and extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation, yet how altered matrix stiffness regulates corneal epithelial dysfunction remains unclear. Here, we established an umbilical cord (UC)-derived decellularized ECM (dECM) hydrogel with adjustable stiffness spanning a broad mechanical spectrum to model the biomechanical features of normal and keratoconic corneas. Using this platform, we revealed that an intermediate, physiological-range stiffness condition most effectively preserved corneal epithelial homeostasis by promoting cytoskeletal organization, reducing apoptosis, and maintaining COL1A1 expression in human corneal epithelial cells. Transcriptomic analysis further revealed that physiological stiffness of the dECM enhanced the expression of genes associated with ECM organization, cell adhesion, and cytoskeletal regulation while suppressing MAPK signaling. Mechanistically, the protective effects of physiological-range dECM were associated with reduced p38/MAPK phosphorylation, altered integrin alpha 5 (ITGA5) expression, and reorganization of F-actin, thereby supporting ECM-related gene expression. Together, these findings identify physiological matrix stiffness as a key regulator of corneal epithelial ECM remodeling and establish a tissue-derived, stiffness-adjustable dECM hydrogel as a relevant platform for studying KC mechanobiology and developing biomaterial-based therapeutic strategies.
